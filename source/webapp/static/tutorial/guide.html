FinSi — User Guide

FinSi answers one question: "Will my money last?" It pulls your accounts, income and spending into one place, tests your plan against thousands of possible market futures, and tells you in plain words what to do next. Everything runs on your own Mac. Your data stays on your computer.

Not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. FinSi gives estimates to help you think and plan. Talk to a qualified professional before making decisions.

1. Getting started and the Setup tab

Video: 1. Setup (about 3 minutes).

Start it

The Mac app: open FinSi from Applications. From source: open Terminal, go to the Code folder and run ./scripts/run_dashboard.sh (to stop it: ./scripts/stop_dashboard.sh). Your browser opens the dashboard. The first time, you see a welcome screen. Create your admin password (at least 8 characters, typed twice) and set up the database: it shows where your data will be saved, and you can keep it somewhere else if you like. Press Create password & set up database. You are then shown a recovery key; save it, because it is the only way back in if you forget the password. Then you land in the setup wizard. This happens once; after that you simply log in.

To try it with made-up data first, open Setup → Sample data and press Load sample household (and Add sample linked accounts for pretend bank and brokerage accounts). Press Remove sample data before you enter your own numbers. After an update, restart the app and refresh the page (Cmd+Shift+R).

Set it up (about 10 minutes)

Step 1: the setup wizard. Open Setup and press Run the setup wizard. It asks, one short step at a time: who the plan is for (your name, your partner's, or turn the second person off), birth years and the year you plan to retire, the growth and inflation you expect (6% and 3% are a reasonable start), and how to bring in your accounts. You can change every answer later.

Step 2: bring in your accounts. Do either or both.

Connect with Plaid gives you live balances, holdings and spending:

  1. Make a free developer account at plaid.com and copy your Client ID and Secret. The sandbox environment has fake test banks, so you can try everything safely. Real banks need Plaid's production environment, which Plaid turns on for your account (check Plaid's current terms).
  2. On Setup → Plaid API keys, paste the Client ID and Secret, choose the environment and press Save. They are stored only in your Mac's Keychain.

  1. On Setup → Connect an account, type a short name (for example wells_fargo_checking), choose what to pull (investments, transactions; tick Full account number for checking or savings) and press Connect. Plaid's secure window opens in a new tab. Log in to your bank there. Your bank password never passes through FinSi.

  1. Repeat for each bank or brokerage. After that, Sync now refreshes everything, and the app also syncs by itself.

Or type it in yourself. Press + Add (bottom right of any page) and choose an asset, a debt, an expense, income or a property. No bank connection is needed.

Brokerage accounts you trade in (Alpaca, Interactive Brokers, ETRADE) connect on the Trading* tab, not here (section 5).

Step 3: tell FinSi how to treat each account. On Assets & Debts, tick In the plan? for the money the plan may spend, and set the Tax treatment (taxable, tax-deferred such as a 401(k), or Roth) and the Owner. A linked account that is not counted yet shows a Classify button on Accounts.

Step 4: protect it. Set a password in Setup → Security, especially before you share your Mac or allow network access. You can add a view-only password too (see below).

The Setup tab

  • Household: names of the people in the plan (a one-person plan works too).
  • Link accounts with Plaid, set a password, and optionally a view-only password.
  • Alerts & weekly email: turn on a weekly summary email and Mac notifications. Enter your email, your email server and an app password (Gmail and iCloud require one; you create it in your account's security settings), and press Send test email. The password is kept in your Mac's Keychain.
  • Backups: a copy of your data is made daily. Press Test latest backup to prove it opens correctly. To go back to an earlier copy, use Restore a backup (it asks for your password and saves your current data first, so a restore can be undone).
  • About, protection and updates: your version, whether your disk is encrypted, Check for updates, and Download support report (a file you can send for help; it leaves out balances, names and passwords).
  • Move to another Mac: see the questions at the end of this guide.
  • Real-money trading limits: the most any single real order may be worth, and the most per day, across all brokers.

Sharing safely: view-only login. Add a view-only password for a spouse, advisor or accountant. They sign in on the same page with that password. They can look at everything and try what-ifs, but cannot change anything, and they do not see the Setup page. Changing or removing the view-only password signs them out immediately.

2. Today — your whole picture

Video: 2. Today (about 2 minutes).

The five tabs. Along the top: Today (this page), My Plan (what you own, owe, earn and spend), Explore (what-ifs and planning tools), Trading (your brokerage accounts, research, the screener and your orders) and Setup. My Plan, Explore and Trading each hold several pages, shown as a second row of buttons under the tabs. Every page keeps its own address, so old bookmarks still work.

Top to bottom, Today shows Alerts (only when something needs a look), whether your plan is on track, Where you stand (progress to independence, safe spending, what changed since last month and which accounts moved most), your net worth over time, a Data check, and What should I do next?, a short numbered list with the most important step first. Every item has an Open link to the right tab.

  • Chance your money lasts. FinSi replays your plan through 1,000 possible good and bad market futures. "982 of 1,000" means your savings lasted to the end in 982 of those 1,000 runs. Aim for about 85% or more.
  • Financial independence. Savings compared with about 25 times the yearly spending your income does not already cover.
  • Safe spending. The most you could spend each year and still keep your chance of lasting at the level you choose.
  • Real numbers. You can see everything in today's dollars or future dollars (a toggle on the Will my money last? page).

The bell at the top right shows how many alerts are open. The eye button blurs every dollar amount, handy when you share your screen.

Alerts appear when your chance of lasting drops 5 points or more in a week (or is low), when a data problem needs fixing, when one holding or sector dominates, when your mix drifts 10+ points from your goal, and when a saved screen you are watching finds new matches. Press Dismiss to hide one; it comes back only if the problem goes away and returns.

Data check looks for things that quietly skew your numbers: a negative cash balance (margin), a fund with the wrong sector tag, accounts that have not synced in a week, holdings with no cost basis, missing or old backups. Some come with a one-click fix.

Watch this video. Every page has an About this page note and a Watch this video button that opens the video for that tab at the right moment. The Tutorial button at the top opens all five videos and this guide.

3. My Plan — what you own, owe, earn and spend

Video: 3. My Plan (about 3 minutes).

Accounts and Positions

Accounts connected through Plaid refresh themselves. Positions lists every holding, with its tax type and cost. Its Portfolio check-up warns if one stock, one sector or one firm holds too much of your money.

Review my holdings. One button on Positions screens every stock, ETF and fund you own, from your linked accounts plus whatever your Alpaca, IBKR and E*TRADE tabs last showed (an account linked both ways is counted once). For each holding it reads the price history and writes a short note, the ones worth a second look first:

  • Worth a look: large and stretched (or large and weakening) — a big share of everything you hold (a single stock over 15%, a fund over 40%) that has also run up fast, is rated Sell, or just crossed down on momentum.
  • Worth a look: weakening — a Sell or Strong Sell rating together with a bearish momentum cross, a big drop over the month, or a deep fall from the 52-week high.
  • Watch, Holding up well and No flags — the rest, with the reasons in plain words (for example "RSI is 76: it has run up fast and is often called overbought").
  • Not enough price history — a ticker no data source covers.

These are prompts to think, not instructions: chart signals know nothing about your goals, taxes or time horizon. Selling a gain in a taxable account is taxed. The review never places an order.

Assets & Debts and Real Estate

Click any number to edit it. Tick In the plan? for the money you want the simulation to spend. Pick a Tax treatment (taxable, tax-deferred like a 401(k), or Roth) and an Owner for each account. Real Estate tracks each property's value, loan, rent and when the loan ends.

Income & Expenses, Actual Spending and Budget vs Actual

Income is listed first, then expenses. Add rows with + Add, tick rows and press Delete selected to remove them. Link a row to a real spending category to see what you actually spent over the last 12 months. The Social Security planner compares claiming ages 62 to 70.

Actual Spending shows what really left your accounts, from your linked transactions. Budget vs Actual puts that next to what you planned.

4. Explore — what could happen, and what could you change

Video: 4. Explore (about 2 minutes).

Will my money last? — the simulation

This is where the chance of lasting comes from. Change the assumptions (growth, inflation, retirement year, tax) or try a what-if such as "spend 10% less" or "retire 2 years later". Nothing is saved until you choose to save it. You can also save named scenarios and compare them.

Risk & Suggestions

Asks one question: how much risk are you comfortable with? Pick Conservative, Moderate, Growth or Aggressive. FinSi turns that into a goal mix of stocks, bonds and cash, compares it with what you hold today (overall and by institution), and tells you what to buy or sell in each account. Selling in retirement accounts is suggested first because it costs no tax. FinSi only suggests; it never places an order.

Drawdown Planning and Legacy Planning

Drawdown Planning shows, year by year, which accounts you draw from, your required minimum distributions (RMDs) and the estimated tax. The card "Which account should I take it from?" compares the usual order (brokerage first) with the split that costs the least tax this year, and shows the tax saved. It looks at one year only, so treat it as a starting point for a conversation with a tax professional.

Legacy Planning compares strategies, such as Roth conversions, for what you could leave your heirs.

5. Trading — brokers, research, screener and orders

Video: 5. Trading (about 5 minutes).

The Trading tab holds six pages: Alpaca, IBKR, E*TRADE, Stock Analysis, Screener and Orders. These are real-money accounts. FinSi never places an order by itself; every order is previewed first and needs your confirmation, and nothing here is investment advice. Balances on these pages are not added to your net worth or plan (link the account through Plaid if you want it counted).

Connecting a broker

  • Alpaca: in your Alpaca dashboard switch to Live, generate an API key and paste both parts into the Connect your Alpaca live account card. The keys are stored in your Mac's Keychain, never in the database.
  • E*TRADE: get a consumer key and secret from E*TRADE's developer page (production), save them, then press Connect and sign in to E*TRADE in the window that opens.
  • IBKR: a small gateway program (it needs Java) runs on your Mac and starts with the app. Press Sign in to IBKR and sign in on IBKR's own page. Your IBKR password goes only to IBKR's gateway, never to FinSi.

Live trading and limits

On Alpaca, live trading is off until you type the confirmation phrase in the Live trading switch; you can have it switch itself off after a few hours or stay on until you turn it off. In Setup → Real-money trading limits you set the most a single order may be worth and the most you may send per day, across all brokers. Orders are sized by whole shares.

One Trade menu per position

Every position has one Trade ▾ button (like E*TRADE's own) with Buy and Sell inside. Choosing one fills in the order ticket with the symbol (and, for a sale, the number of shares) and the right account. Each position also shows a rating pill from Strong Buy (green) to Strong Sell (red), worked out from trend, momentum and averages.

Preview and safety checks

Press Preview order and you see the estimated cost and a list of plain-language safety checks before anything is sent: how big the order is next to the account (a warning at 25%), whether a buy would make one stock more than 15% of the account, whether a sale sells more than you hold or your whole position, a sale below your average cost with a wash-sale reminder, a recent sale of the same stock from this app, a limit price far from the market, extended-hours and wide-spread warnings. Checks are heads-ups; the size limits are what block an order. To send it you type the symbol again, then confirm.

E*TRADE and IBKR

They share one layout: accounts, positions with the Trade menu, open orders and the preview. On E*TRADE and IBKR, click the arrow beside a position to see its tax lots: what each batch of shares cost, the gain, and whether it is short or long term. Which lot is sold depends on the lot method set in your brokerage account; the API cannot pick a lot, so FinSi shows the lots but does not choose one.

Stock Analysis

Research only. Look up any stock or ETF for its price, day and 52-week range, chart, trend, a buy / sell rating with the signals behind it, news and earnings. Keep a watchlist with notes of your own. Nothing here buys or sells.

Screener

Describe what you want in plain English ("technology stocks that moved at least 5% this month", "ETFs where MACD crossed up in the last 3 days and RSI is under 40") and the screener turns it into filters you can change, then scans about 600 big stocks and popular ETFs, your holdings or your watchlist. Filters cover MACD crossovers, RSI, price swings in percent or dollars, sectors and themes, volume, put/call volume, rating and more. Prices come from Alpaca's free feed (Yahoo Finance for anything it lacks); put/call volume comes from E*TRADE when you are signed in and from Yahoo otherwise.

  • Sort by rating (Strong Buy first or Strong Sell first), biggest move, RSI, volume and more. Ratings use the same green-to-red pills as everywhere else.
  • Trade ▾ on every row opens the order form for the account you pick, with the symbol filled in. Nothing is sent from the screener.
  • Tick rows to add them to your watchlist, Download CSV, and use Columns to choose what to show.
  • Saved screens: press Save screen, and tick tell me about new matches. The app re-checks it once a day while it is open and shows new names in the alerts bell (and as a Mac notification if you turned those on).

Searching is not advice: a screen shows what matched a rule, not what to buy.

Orders

Every real-money order sent from the app, across all three brokers, newest first. Filter by days, broker or side, see today's total against your daily limit, and Download CSV. Alpaca statuses are refreshed from Alpaca; for IBKR and E*TRADE the status is the last one the broker reported when the order was sent, so open that tab for the live order list.

6. Privacy and safety

  • Your data lives in a database on your Mac (data/retirement_plan.db, or the folder you chose at first launch), plus backups you can restore from inside the app.
  • Plaid, Alpaca, E*TRADE and email passwords and keys are stored in the macOS Keychain.
  • The app listens only on your Mac unless you change Network access in Setup, and then a password is required.
  • FinSi never moves money. It places an order only when you press the final confirm button on a Trading page after a preview and typing the symbol, within the limits you set.

7. Common questions

Why does my chance of lasting change from day to day? Markets are simulated randomly, and your balances, spending and assumptions change. FinSi keeps its test runs consistent for the same inputs, so a change usually means something real changed. Look at "Since last month" on Today.

It says my mix is off but I hold bond funds. Funds are sorted by name and tag. If one is wrong, fix its tag in Positions or use the Data check's Clear these tags button.

Can I see everything in today's dollars? Yes. Use the dollars toggle on the Will my money last? page.

I forgot my admin password. On the login page press Forgot your password? and type the recovery key you saved at first launch. You choose a new password and get a new recovery key. If you lost the recovery key too, there is no way back in from inside the app, because that is what keeps your numbers private. Your data file is still on your Mac, and backups sit next to it. While you are still signed in, Setup → Security → Make a new recovery key replaces a lost key.

Is my data safe? It stays on your Mac, readable only by your user account, and the app sends nothing out on its own. Turn on FileVault (System Settings → Privacy & Security) so the disk is encrypted if the Mac is lost. See Privacy & terms at the bottom of any page.

How do I get a newer version? Setup → About, protection and updates → Check for updates. If something goes wrong, Download support report makes a file you can send for help.

How do I move to a new Mac? On the old Mac go to Setup → Move to another Mac, type your password and choose a passphrase for the file, and press Save transfer file. It holds your bank connections, login password, recovery key, data and backups, locked with that passphrase. Move the file to the new Mac (AirDrop or a USB stick), open the app, and on the welcome screen choose Moving from another Mac?. Pick the file, type the passphrase, and log in with your old password. Delete the file afterwards.

How do I start over? Setup → Sample data → Remove sample data removes only the sample rows. Your own rows stay.

The Mac app asks about a broker I do not use. Nothing is required: Alpaca, IBKR and E*TRADE are optional, and their pages simply show a connect card until you add keys.

Made-up sample data is used in all screenshots and videos. Not financial, investment, tax or legal advice.
