FinSi answers one question: "Will my money last?" It pulls your accounts, income and spending into one place, tests your plan against thousands of possible market futures, and tells you in plain words what to do next. Everything runs on your own Mac. Your data stays on your computer.

Not financial, investment, tax or legal advice. FinSi gives estimates to help you think and plan. Talk to a qualified professional before making decisions.

1. Start it

  1. Open Terminal and go to the Code folder.
  2. Run ./scripts/run_dashboard.sh (to stop it: ./scripts/stop_dashboard.sh).
  3. Your browser opens the dashboard. The first time, you see a welcome screen (the Mac app does the same). Create your admin password (at least 8 characters, typed twice) and set up the database: it shows where your data will be saved, and you can keep it somewhere else if you like. Press Create password & set up database. You are then shown a recovery key; save it, because it is the only way back in if you forget the password. Then you land in the setup wizard. This happens once; after that you simply log in.

  1. To try it with made-up data first, open Setup → Sample data and press Load sample household (and Add sample linked accounts for pretend bank and brokerage accounts). Press Remove sample data before you enter your own numbers.

After an update, restart the dashboard and refresh the page (Cmd+Shift+R).

2. Set it up (about 10 minutes)

Step 1: the setup wizard. Open Setup and press Run the setup wizard. It asks, one short step at a time: who the plan is for (your name, your partner's, or turn the second person off), birth years and the year you plan to retire, the growth and inflation you expect (6% and 3% are a reasonable start), and how to bring in your accounts. You can change every answer later.

Meet Fin. A small robot guide sits at the top of the wizard. It says what each step is for in a speech bubble, and it can say it out loud: press Hear this to listen again, or Voice on to switch the voice off (your choice is remembered). Fin also narrates the tutorial video.

Step 2: bring in your accounts. Do either or both.

Connect with Plaid gives you live balances, holdings and spending:

  1. Make a free developer account at plaid.com and copy your Client ID and Secret. The sandbox environment has fake test banks, so you can try everything safely. Real banks need Plaid's production environment, which Plaid turns on for your account (check Plaid's current terms).
  2. On Setup → Plaid API keys, paste the Client ID and Secret, choose the environment and press Save. They are stored only in your Mac's Keychain.

  1. On Setup → Connect an account, type a short name (for example wells_fargo_checking), choose what to pull (investments, transactions; tick Full account number for checking or savings) and press Connect. Plaid's secure window opens in a new tab. Log in to your bank there. Your bank password never passes through FinSi.

  1. Repeat for each bank or brokerage. After that, Sync now refreshes everything, and the app also syncs by itself.

Or type it in yourself. Press + Add (bottom right of any page) and choose an asset, a debt, an expense, income or a property. No bank connection is needed.

Step 3: tell FinSi how to treat each account. On Assets, tick In the plan? for the money the plan may spend, and set the Tax treatment (taxable, tax-deferred such as a 401(k), or Roth) and the Owner. A linked account that is not counted yet shows a Classify button on Linked Accounts.

Step 4: protect it. Set a password in Setup → Security, especially before you share your Mac or allow network access. You can add a view-only password too (section 6).

Just looking around? Press Load sample household in the wizard or on Setup to fill in a made-up family, and Remove sample data when you are ready for your own.

3. The big idea in 60 seconds

  • Chance your money lasts. FinSi replays your plan through 1,000 possible good and bad market futures. "982 of 1,000" means your savings lasted to the end in 982 of those 1,000 runs. Aim for about 85% or more.
  • Financial independence. Savings compared with about 25 times the yearly spending your income does not already cover.
  • Safe spending. The most you could spend each year and still keep your chance of lasting at the level you choose.
  • Real numbers. You can see everything in today's dollars or future dollars (a toggle on the Will my money last? tab).

4. A tour of every tab

Not sure what something means? Tap the small i next to any tile or card title and a pop-up explains it in plain words: what it is, how to read it and what you can do. Every tab also has an About this page button at the top right.

Summary — your whole picture

Top to bottom: Alerts (only when something needs a look), whether your plan is on track, Where you stand (progress to independence, safe spending, what changed since last month and which accounts moved most), your net worth over time, a Data check, and What should I do next?, a short numbered list with the most important step first. Every item has an Open link to the right tab.

The bell at the top right shows how many alerts are open. The eye button blurs every dollar amount, handy when you share your screen.

Assets and Real Estate — what you own and owe

Click any number to edit it. Tick In the plan? for the money you want the simulation to spend. Pick a Tax treatment (taxable, tax-deferred like a 401(k), or Roth) and an Owner for each account. Real Estate tracks each property's value, loan, rent and when the loan ends.

Linked Accounts — Accounts, Positions, Risk & Suggestions

Accounts connected through Plaid refresh themselves. Positions lists every holding. Its Portfolio check-up warns if one stock, one sector or one firm holds too much of your money.

Risk & Suggestions asks one question: how much risk are you comfortable with? Pick Conservative, Moderate, Growth or Aggressive. FinSi turns that into a goal mix of stocks, bonds and cash, compares it with what you hold today (overall and by institution), and tells you what to buy or sell in each account. Selling in retirement accounts is suggested first because it costs no tax. FinSi only suggests; it never places an order.

Income & Expenses — what comes in and goes out

Income is listed first, then expenses. Add rows with + Add, tick rows and press Delete selected to remove them. Link a row to a real spending category to see what you actually spent over the last 12 months. The Social Security rows there are set by the planner on the Will my money last? tab and cannot be edited here.

Spending (under Actual Spending and Budget vs Actual) shows what really left your accounts, from your linked transactions.

Will my money last? — the simulation

This is where the chance of lasting comes from. Change the assumptions (growth, inflation, retirement year, tax) or try a what-if such as "spend 10% less" or "retire 2 years later". Nothing is saved until you choose to save it. You can also save named scenarios and compare them.

Drawdown & Legacy

Drawdown Planning shows, year by year, which accounts you draw from, your required minimum distributions (RMDs) and the estimated tax. The card "Which account should I take it from?" compares the usual order (brokerage first) with the split that costs the least tax this year, and shows the tax saved. It looks at one year only, so treat it as a starting point for a conversation with a tax professional.

Legacy Planning compares strategies, such as Roth conversions, for what you could leave your heirs.

Setup

  • Household: names of the people in the plan (a one-person plan works too).
  • Link accounts with Plaid, set a password, and optionally a view-only password (see section 6).
  • Alerts & weekly email: turn on a weekly summary email and Mac notifications.
  • Backups: a copy of your data is made daily. Press Test latest backup to prove it opens correctly. To go back to an earlier copy, use Restore a backup (it asks for your password and saves your current data first, so a restore can be undone).

4b. Quick tour and What if...?

Every tab and tool is always shown. Long explanations sit behind a small i next to each title. The Take a quick tour button on the Summary page walks through the main tabs.

What if...? on the Summary lets you tap a change, such as spending 10% less or retiring 2 years later, and see how your chance of lasting changes. Nothing is saved.

5. Alerts, Data check and next steps

  • Alerts appear when your chance of lasting drops 5 points or more in a week (or is low), when a data problem needs fixing, when one holding or sector dominates, or when your mix drifts 10+ points from your goal. Press Dismiss to hide one; it comes back only if the problem goes away and returns.
  • Data check looks for things that quietly skew your numbers: a negative cash balance (margin), a fund with the wrong sector tag, accounts that have not synced in a week, holdings with no cost basis, missing or old backups. Some come with a one-click fix.
  • Weekly email: in Setup → Alerts & weekly email enter your email, your email server and an app password (Gmail and iCloud require one; you create it in your account's security settings). Press Send test email. The password is kept in your Mac's Keychain, never in the database.

6. Sharing safely: view-only login

Your admin password was created on first launch (change it any time in Setup → Security). Then add a view-only password for a spouse, advisor or accountant. They sign in on the same page with that password. They can look at everything and try what-ifs, but cannot change anything, and they do not see the Setup page. Changing or removing the view-only password signs them out immediately.

7. Privacy and safety

  • Your data lives in a database on your Mac (data/retirement_plan.db), plus backups you can restore from inside the app.
  • Plaid passwords and email passwords are stored in the macOS Keychain.
  • The app listens only on your Mac unless you change Network access in Setup, and then a password is required.
  • FinSi never moves money or places trades.

8. Common questions

Why does my chance of lasting change from day to day? Markets are simulated randomly, and your balances, spending and assumptions change. FinSi keeps its test runs consistent for the same inputs, so a change usually means something real changed. Look at "Since last month" on the Summary.

It says my mix is off but I hold bond funds. Funds are sorted by name and tag. If one is wrong, fix its tag in Positions or use the Data check's Clear these tags button.

Can I see everything in today's dollars? Yes. Use the dollars toggle on the Will my money last? tab.

I forgot my admin password. On the login page press Forgot your password? and type the recovery key you saved at first launch. You choose a new password and get a new recovery key. If you lost the recovery key too, there is no way back in from inside the app, because that is what keeps your numbers private. Your data file is still on your Mac (data/retirement_plan.db, or the folder you chose), and backups sit next to it. While you are still signed in, Setup → Security → Make a new recovery key replaces a lost key.

Is my data safe? It stays on your Mac, readable only by your user account, and the app sends nothing out on its own. Turn on FileVault (System Settings → Privacy & Security) so the disk is encrypted if the Mac is lost. See Privacy & terms at the bottom of any page.

How do I get a newer version? Setup → About, protection and updates → Check for updates. If something goes wrong, Download support report makes a file you can send for help. It leaves out balances, names and passwords.

How do I move to a new Mac? On the old Mac go to Setup → Move to another Mac, type your password and choose a passphrase for the file, and press Save transfer file. It holds your bank connections, login password, recovery key, data and backups, locked with that passphrase. Move the file to the new Mac (AirDrop or a USB stick), open the app, and on the welcome screen choose Moving from another Mac?. Pick the file, type the passphrase, and log in with your old password. Delete the file afterwards. From Terminal on a source install the same thing is venv/bin/python scripts/transfer.py export and import FILE.

The app opens a page that says "refused to connect", or closes by itself. That means the app's background program did not start. Open the app again: it now clears the macOS "downloaded from the internet" mark by itself and, if something is still wrong, shows a message saying why. If you copied the app from another Mac, copy the whole app with Finder or the .dmg (not a partial folder), and build it on the Mac type you will use (Apple chip or Intel). For a quick manual fix, open Terminal and run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/FinSi.app, then right-click the app and choose Open. The reason is always written to ~/Library/Application Support/RetirementPlan/logs/launch.log; Setup → Download support report includes the end of it. "Bad CPU type in executable" in the log means the app was built for the other kind of Mac (Apple chip vs Intel). Build it on the Mac you will use, or build once on each kind. An Intel-built app can also run on an Apple-chip Mac after Rosetta is installed (the app offers to do that).

A holding with a negative value is usually a margin loan (money borrowed against the account). On Linked Positions press Mark as margin loan on that row. It then stops showing as a warning, and Risk & Suggestions shows how much is borrowed, what you own after the loan, and how much a market drop would hurt. The stock/bond/cash mix is worked out on your holdings before the loan, so it shows what you are really exposed to. The loan is already inside the account's balance on Linked Accounts, so do not add it again under Liabilities.

Connecting a bank says "certificate verify failed". The Mac does not trust the secure connection, which usually means a work or school network, VPN or security filter that inspects web traffic. FinSi now trusts the same certificates your browser does, so update to the latest version first (rebuild the Mac app, or restart the from-source copy; it installs what it needs). If it still fails, try another network such as your phone's hotspot, or turn the filter off.

Where do I type my Social Security amount? In the Social Security card on the Will my money last? tab. Type the monthly amount at full retirement age exactly as your ssa.gov statement shows it (for example 3,500; commas and a $ sign are fine), choose when each of you claims, and press Save; it says Saved. It turns the monthly amount into a yearly one and writes the Social Security rows; those rows are replaced each time you apply, so change the amount in the planner rather than editing the row.

How do I start over? Setup → Sample data → Remove sample data removes only the sample rows. Your own rows stay.
